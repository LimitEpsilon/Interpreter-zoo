\section{A Denotational Semantics for a Module Language}

This document defines a denotational semantics for a call-by-value language with first-class modules and recursive bindings.
It proves that the semantics agrees with an interpreter.
The semantics is computable, and it uses no coinductive type.
The directory \rocq{denot/} holds the definitions and the proofs in Rocq.
Table~\ref{tab:rocq} gives the Rocq name of each statement.

The terms of this document are the terms of \rocq{denot/glossary.md}.
The proofs use the structured style of L.~Lamport, \emph{How to Write a Proof}.

\subsection{The Language}

    Constructor & $C$ & $\in$ & $\Cstr$                                          &                  \\

                &     &       & $\vbar$ $e\synlink e$ $\vbar$ $\varepsilon$ $\vbar$ $\synbind{x}{e}{e}$ & link term, empty module, binding \\
                &     &       & $\vbar$ $C\:\overline{e}$ $\vbar$ $\texttt{match}\:e\:\{\overline{C\:\overline{x}\rightarrow e}\}$ & constructor, match
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Terms.}
  \label{fig:syntax}
\end{figure}

A module is a value that holds an environment.
The link term $e_1\synlink e_2$ evaluates $e_2$ in the environment of the module of $e_1$.
The binding $\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}$ binds $x$ in $e_2$ and in $e_1$.
It gives the module of $e_2$ with $x$ in front.
Thus a binding defines a recursive function, a recursive functor, or mutually recursive modules.

\subsection{Semantic Domains}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \small
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{rrcl}
    Anchor      & $a$      & $\in$ & $\Anchor\Coloneqq\bidx{i}$ $\vbar$ $\ell$                                              \\
    Shadow      & $S$      & $\in$ & $\Event\Coloneqq\InitE$ $\vbar$ $\ReadE(S,x)$ $\vbar$ $\CallE(S,v)$ $\vbar$ $\FldE(S,C,i)$ \\
    Environment & $\sigma$ & $\in$ & $\Ctx\Coloneqq\bullet$ $\vbar$ $[S]$ $\vbar$ $(x,\omega)\cons\sigma$                       \\
    Weak value  & $\omega$ & $\in$ & $\Walue\Coloneqq v$ $\vbar$ $\muval{v}$ $\vbar$ $a$                                       \\
    Value       & $v$      & $\in$ & $\Value\Coloneqq\sigma$ $\vbar$ $S$ $\vbar$ $\langle\lambda x.b,\sigma\rangle$ $\vbar$ $C\:\overline{v}$ \\
    Body        & $b$      & $\in$ & $\Body\triangleq\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\Trace$                                            \\
    Trace       & $t$      & $\in$ & $\Trace\Coloneqq\bot$ $\vbar$ $v$ $\vbar$ $\guardt{v}{t}$ $\vbar$ $\MatchT\:S\:\{\overline{C/n\Mapsto t}\}$
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Semantic domains. Each domain is inductive.}
  \label{fig:domains}
\end{figure}

An anchor is an index $\bidx{i}$ or a name $\ell$.
A value is a module, a shadow, a closure or a constructor value.
A trace is $\bot$, a leaf, a guard or a branch node.

A shadow is a computation that waits for the unknown environment $\InitE$.
The shadow $\ReadE(S,x)$ reads the variable $x$ of the module $S$.
The shadow $\CallE(S,v)$ applies the function $S$ to $v$.
The shadow $\FldE(S,C,i)$ is field $i$ of $S$, when $S$ is a constructor value of $C$.
The environment $[S]$ is a module that is a shadow.

A closure holds a body, not a term.
A body is one trace for each fuel.
A trace is a finite tree, and $\bot$ gives no information.
The guard $\guardt{v}{t}$ says that linking must resolve $v$ before $t$ counts.
A branch node has one trace for each branch of a match on the shadow $S$.
The spine of a trace is its guards and its scrutinees, without its leaves.

An entry $(x,\omega)$ of an environment binds the variable $x$ to the weak value $\omega$.
A weak value is a value, a recursive value $\muval{v}$ or an anchor.
Only a value is the result of a term.

An anchor is the placeholder for the value of a binding while the right side of the binding runs.
Anchors are locally nameless.
A recursive value around an index binds the index, and a name is free.
The index $\bidx{0}$ in $v$ is the anchor of the recursive value $\muval{v}$.
The name $\dead$ is the dead anchor.

\subsection{Operations on Anchors}\label{sec:ops}

The operations of this section are structural recursions on values.
They do not change the body of a closure.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Open: $\openloc{\ell}{v}$ is $v$ with the index $\bidx{0}$ made the name $\ell$.
  \item Close: $\closeloc{\ell}{v}$ is $v$ with the name $\ell$ made the index $\bidx{0}$.
  \item Rename: $\xi(v)$ is $v$ with each name $\ell$ made $\xi(\ell)$, for a function $\xi$ on names.
  \item Replace: $\repl{v}{u}{\ell}$ is $v$ with each weak value $\ell$ made $\muval{u}$.
  \item Unroll: $\unroll(\muval{v})$ is $v$ with each weak value $\bidx{0}$ made $\muval{v}$.
\end{itemize}

A name is fresh for a value when no entry of the value holds it.
$\fresh(\sigma,v)$ is a name that is fresh for $\sigma$ and for $v$, and that is not $\dead$.
A value is locally closed when a recursive value around each index binds the index.
A value is well formed when it is locally closed and each body of a closure is the denotation of a term.

The value $\forcef(\omega)$ of a weak value is a partial function:
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \forcef(v)          & \triangleq v                 & \qquad \forcef(\muval{v}) & \triangleq\unroll(\muval{v})
\end{alignat*}
The value of an anchor is not defined.

The read of a variable in an environment is a partial function:
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  [S](x)                         & \triangleq\ReadE(S,x)          &                                \\
  ((x,\omega)\cons\sigma)(x)     & \triangleq\forcef(\omega)      &                                \\
  ((y,\omega)\cons\sigma)(x)     & \triangleq\sigma(x)            & \qquad\text{when }y\neq x
\end{alignat*}
Thus the read of a variable that is bound to an anchor is not defined.
The read of a variable in $\bullet$ is not defined.

\subsection{Traces}

The trace $t\bindt k$ is $t$ with the trace $k(v)$ in the place of each leaf $v$.
A trace is finished when it has only guards and then one leaf.
$\result(t)$ is the value of that leaf, and $\cost(t)$ is the number of the guards.
The trace $t'$ extends $t$, written $t\ext t'$, when $t'$ is $t$ with a trace in the place of some $\bot$.
$\killf{\ell}(t)$ is $t$ with the name $\ell$ made $\dead$ in each value of the spine.
The leaves of $\killf{\ell}(t)$ are the leaves of $t$.

A statement about a fuel $m$ holds \emph{from some fuel} when a fuel $m_0$ exists such that the statement holds for each $m\ge m_0$.

\subsection{Linking}

Linking puts an environment $\sigma_0$ in the place of the unknown environment, and runs each shadow.
$\sigma_0\lk{n}X$ links $\sigma_0$ into a shadow, an environment, a value or a trace $X$ at the fuel $n$.
$\sigma_0\splk{n}t$ links $\sigma_0$ into the spine of $t$.
$\runb{n}(\sigma,b)$ links $\sigma$ into the body $b$, and $\lnkv{n}(\sigma_0,v)$ links $\sigma_0$ into the value $v$ of a recursive value.
The fuel bounds the depth of the calls and of the recursive values.

\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \runb{0}(\sigma,b)       & \triangleq\bot   & \qquad \runb{n+1}(\sigma,b)     & \triangleq\sigma\lk{n}b(n)  \\
  \lnkv{0}(\sigma_0,v)     & \triangleq\bot   & \qquad \lnkv{n+1}(\sigma_0,v)   & \triangleq\sigma_0\lk{n}v   \\
  \callt{n}(\sigma,b)      & \triangleq\guardt{\sigma}{\runb{n}(\sigma,b)}
\end{alignat*}

\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \InitE                              &  & \triangleq\sigma_0                                                                                          \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \ReadE(S,x)                         &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}S)\bindt\rdf{x}                                                                     \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \CallE(S,v)                         &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}S)\bindt\lambda f.(\sigma_0\lk{n}v)\bindt\lambda a.\apf{n}(f,a)                     \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \FldE(S,C,i)                        &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}S)\bindt\fldf{C}{i}                                                                 \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \bullet                             &  & \triangleq\bullet                                                                                            \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & [S]                                 &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}S)\bindt\modf                                                                       \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & (x,v)\cons\sigma                    &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}v)\bindt\lambda v'.(\sigma_0\lk{n}\sigma)\bindt\lambda\sigma'.(x,v')\cons\sigma'    \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & (x,\muval{v})\cons\sigma            &  & \triangleq\killf{\ell}(\lnkv{n}(\sigma_0,\openloc{\ell}{v}))\bindt\lambda v'.                              \\
                   &                                     &  & \qquad(\sigma_0\lk{n}\sigma)\bindt\lambda\sigma'.(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{v'}})\cons\sigma'
                   \qquad\text{where }\ell=\fresh(\sigma_0,v)                                                                    \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & (x,a)\cons\sigma                    &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}\sigma)\bindt\lambda\sigma'.(x,a)\cons\sigma'                                        \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & \langle\lambda x.b,\sigma\rangle    &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}\sigma)\bindt\lambda\sigma'.\langle\lambda x.b,\sigma'\rangle                        \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{} & C\:v_1\dots v_k                     &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}v_1)\bindt\lambda v_1'.\cdots(\sigma_0\lk{n}v_k)\bindt\lambda v_k'.C\:v_1'\dots v_k'
\end{alignat*}

The equation of an entry $(x,\omega)\cons\sigma$ has 1 case for each form of the weak value $\omega$.
Linking into a weak value alone is not a trace, because a leaf of a trace is a value.

The continuations read a variable, apply a function, take a field and take a module.
In each case that the equations do not give, the trace is $\bot$.
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \rdf{x}(\sigma)                               & \triangleq\guardt{\sigma}{\sigma(x)}                 & \qquad \rdf{x}(S)       & \triangleq\ReadE(S,x)   \\
  \apf{n}(\langle\lambda x.b,\sigma\rangle,a)   & \triangleq\callt{n}((x,a)\cons\sigma,b)              & \qquad \apf{n}(S,a)     & \triangleq\CallE(S,a)   \\
  \fldf{C}{i}(C\:\overline{v})                  & \triangleq\guardt{C\:\overline{v}}{v_i}              & \qquad \fldf{C}{i}(S)   & \triangleq\FldE(S,C,i)  \\
  \modf(\sigma)                                 & \triangleq\sigma                                     & \qquad \modf(S)         & \triangleq[S]
\end{alignat*}

Linking into a trace links into the spine, and then into the leaf.
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \sigma_0\splk{n}{} & \bot                               &  & \triangleq\bot                                                                           \\
  \sigma_0\splk{n}{} & v                                  &  & \triangleq v                                                                             \\
  \sigma_0\splk{n}{} & (\guardt{u}{t})                    &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}u)\bindt\lambda u'.\guardt{u'}{(\sigma_0\splk{n}t)}              \\
  \sigma_0\splk{n}{} & \MatchT\:S\:\{\overline{C/k\Mapsto t}\} &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\lk{n}S)\bindt\mathit{sel}                                          \\
                     &                                    &  & \qquad\text{where }\mathit{sel}(S')=\MatchT\:S'\:\{\overline{C/k\Mapsto\sigma_0\splk{n}t}\} \\
                     &                                    &  & \qquad\text{and }\mathit{sel}(C_j\:\overline{v})=\guardt{C_j\:\overline{v}}{(\sigma_0\splk{n}t_j)}\text{ when }|\overline{v}|=k_j \\
  \sigma_0\lk{n}{}   & t                                  &  & \triangleq(\sigma_0\splk{n}t)\bindt\lambda v.\sigma_0\lk{n}v
\end{alignat*}

\paragraph{Guards.}
When linking uses only a part of a resolved value, it keeps the whole value in a guard.
The cases are the read of a module, the field of a constructor value, the branch of a match and the environment of a body.
A later linking must then resolve each value that a call-by-value run computes.

\paragraph{Recursive values.}
To link into a recursive value, linking opens it with a fresh anchor, links, and closes it.
The anchor then leaves its scope.
So linking kills the anchor in the spine.
The opened value is not a structural part of the recursive value.
Thus this case uses the fuel, through $\lnkv{n}$.

\subsection{The Denotation}

The denotation $\sembracket{e}$ of a term is a body.
$\den{e}{n}$ is its trace at the fuel $n$.
It is a structural recursion on $e$, and it takes no environment.
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \den{x}{n}                      & \triangleq\ReadE(\InitE,x)                                                                                    \\
  \den{\lambda x.e}{n}            & \triangleq\langle\lambda x.\sembracket{e},[\InitE]\rangle                                                      \\
  \den{e_1\:e_2}{n}               & \triangleq\den{e_1}{n}\bindt\lambda f.\den{e_2}{n}\bindt\lambda a.\apf{n}(f,a)                                 \\
  \den{e_1\synlink e_2}{n}        & \triangleq\den{e_1}{n}\bindt\lambda m.\callt{n}(\modf(m),\sembracket{e_2})                                     \\
  \den{\varepsilon}{n}            & \triangleq\bullet                                                                                             \\
  \den{\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}}{n}  & \triangleq\killf{1}(\callt{n}((x,1)\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e_1}))\bindt\lambda v.                              \\
                                  & \qquad\callt{n}((x,\muval{\closeloc{1}{v}})\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e_2})\bindt\lambda m.(x,\muval{\closeloc{1}{v}})\cons\modf(m) \\
  \den{C\:e_1\dots e_k}{n}        & \triangleq\den{e_1}{n}\bindt\lambda v_1.\cdots\den{e_k}{n}\bindt\lambda v_k.C\:v_1\dots v_k                   \\
  \den{\texttt{match}\:e\:\{\overline{C\:\overline{x}\rightarrow e'}\}}{n} & \triangleq\den{e}{n}\bindt\mathit{sel}                         \\
                                  & \qquad\text{where }\mathit{sel}(C_j\:\overline{v})=\callt{n}((\overline{x_j},\overline{v})\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e_j'})\text{ when }|\overline{v}|=|\overline{x_j}| \\
                                  & \qquad\text{and }\mathit{sel}(S)=\MatchT\:S\:\{\overline{C/|\overline{x}|\Mapsto\callt{n}((\overline{x},\overline{\FldE(S,C,i)})\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e'})}\}
\end{alignat*}
A match on a constructor value takes the first branch with the name and the number of fields of the value.
The result of a term $e$ in an environment $\sigma$ at the fuel $n$ is $\result(\runb{n}(\sigma,\sembracket{e}))$.

The denotation of a binding links $(x,1)\cons[\InitE]$ into the denotation of $e_1$.
It closes the anchor $1$ in the result, which gives the recursive value of $x$.
Then it continues with $e_2$.

\subsection{The Reference Interpreter}

The reference interpreter has environments and fuel.
It has no shadows, no traces and no linking, and a closure holds a term.
\begin{center}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{rrcl}
    Environment & $\rho$   & $\Coloneqq$ & $\bullet$ $\vbar$ $(x,\omega)\cons\rho$                                  \\
    Weak value  & $\omega$ & $\Coloneqq$ & $u$ $\vbar$ $\muval{u}$ $\vbar$ $a$                                     \\
    Value       & $u$      & $\Coloneqq$ & $\rho$ $\vbar$ $\langle\lambda x.e,\rho\rangle$ $\vbar$ $C\:\overline{u}$
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}
$\evalf{N}(e,\rho)$ is the value of $e$ in $\rho$ at the fuel $N$.
It is a partial function, and $\evalf{0}$ is not defined.
\begin{alignat*}{2}
  \evalf{N+1}(x,\rho)                    & \triangleq\rho(x)                                                                                                   \\
  \evalf{N+1}(\lambda x.e,\rho)          & \triangleq\langle\lambda x.e,\rho\rangle                                                                            \\
  \evalf{N+1}(e_1\:e_2,\rho)             & \triangleq\evalf{N}(e,(x,u_2)\cons\rho_1)                                                                            \\
                                         & \qquad\text{where }\evalf{N}(e_1,\rho)=\langle\lambda x.e,\rho_1\rangle\text{ and }\evalf{N}(e_2,\rho)=u_2            \\
  \evalf{N+1}(e_1\synlink e_2,\rho)      & \triangleq\evalf{N}(e_2,\rho_1)\qquad\text{where }\evalf{N}(e_1,\rho)=\rho_1                                         \\
  \evalf{N+1}(\varepsilon,\rho)          & \triangleq\bullet                                                                                                   \\
  \evalf{N+1}(\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2},\rho) & \triangleq(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho_2                                                               \\
                                         & \qquad\text{where }\ell=\fresh(\rho),\ \evalf{N}(e_1,(x,\ell)\cons\rho)=u_1                                           \\
                                         & \qquad\text{and }\evalf{N}(e_2,(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho)=\rho_2
\end{alignat*}
A constructor evaluates its arguments from left to right.
A match evaluates its scrutinee, and then the first branch with the name and the number of fields of the value.
The read $\rho(x)$ is the value of the weak value of $x$, as in Section~\ref{sec:ops}.
$\fresh(\rho)$ is a name that is fresh for $\rho$ and that is not $\dead$.

The lift $\lift{u}$ of a value of the interpreter is $u$ with $\sembracket{e}$ in the place of the term $e$ of each closure.
An environment $\rho$ is locally closed when $\lift{\rho}$ is locally closed.

\subsubsection{Fuel}

\begin{lem}[More fuel]\label{lem:fuel}
  Let $n\le m$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\runb{n}(\sigma,b)\ext\runb{m}(\sigma,b)$, $\lnkv{n}(\sigma,v)\ext\lnkv{m}(\sigma,v)$ and $\den{e}{n}\ext\den{e}{m}$.
    \item If $t$ is finished and $t\ext t'$, then $t'=t$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \step{f1}{If $t\ext t'$, and $k(v)\ext k'(v)$ for each $v$, then $t\bindt k\ext t'\bindt k'$.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $t$.
  \end{proof}
  \step{f2}{Assume that $\callt{n}(\sigma,b)\ext\callt{n+1}(\sigma,b)$ and $\lnkv{n}(\sigma,v)\ext\lnkv{n+1}(\sigma,v)$ for each $\sigma$, $b$ and $v$.
    Then $\sigma_0\lk{n}X\ext\sigma_0\lk{n+1}X'$ for each $X\ext X'$, and $\den{e}{n}\ext\den{e}{n+1}$.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $X$, and on $e$.
    Each case is a use of \stepref{f1}.
    The function $\killf{\ell}$ is monotone.
  \end{proof}
  \step{f3}{Statement 1 holds.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $n$.
    At the fuel $0$ the traces are $\bot$.
    The step is \stepref{f2}.
  \end{proof}
  \step{f4}{Statement 2 holds.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $t$.
    A finished trace has no $\bot$, and $\ext$ keeps each value.
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By \stepref{f3} and \stepref{f4}.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}, more fuel does not change a finished trace.
So a result at one fuel is the result at each larger fuel.

\subsubsection{Anchors}\label{sec:anchors}

\begin{lem}[Linking adds no free anchor]\label{lem:fresh}
  Let $\ell\neq\dead$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\ell$ is fresh for $\sigma_0$ and for $X$, then $\ell$ is fresh for each value of $\sigma_0\lk{n}X$.
    \item If $\ell$ is fresh for $\sigma$, then $\ell$ is fresh for each value of $\runb{n}(\sigma,b)$.
    \item $\ell$ is fresh for each value of $\den{e}{n}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \pfsketch\ The proof is an induction on $n$.
  The 2 cases that are not structural are the recursive value and the binding.
  \step{n1}{\assume{Statements 1 and 2 hold for $\lnkv{n}$ and $\runb{n}$.}\prove{Statement 1 holds for $\sigma_0\lk{n}(x,\muval{v})\cons\sigma$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \step{n11}{\pflet{$\ell'=\fresh(\sigma_0,v)$ and $T=\lnkv{n}(\sigma_0,\openloc{\ell'}{v})$.}}
    \step{n12}{\case{$\ell=\ell'$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ $\killf{\ell}$ takes $\ell$ out of the spine of $T$.
      For each leaf $v'$ of $T$, $\ell$ is fresh for $\closeloc{\ell}{v'}$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{n13}{\case{$\ell\neq\ell'$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ $\ell$ is fresh for $\openloc{\ell'}{v}$.
      By the assumption, $\ell$ is fresh for each value of $T$.
      $\killf{\ell'}$ and the close of $\ell'$ keep $\ell$ fresh, because $\ell\neq\dead$.
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By \stepref{n12} and \stepref{n13}.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \step{n2}{\assume{Statement 2 holds for $\runb{n}$.}\prove{Statement 3 holds for $\den{\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}}{n}$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ As in \stepref{n1}, with the anchor $1$ in the place of $\ell'$.
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $n$.
    Statement 3 at $n$ uses statement 2 at $n$ and \stepref{n2}.
    Statements 1 and 2 at $n+1$ use the statements at $n$ and \stepref{n1}.
    Each other case is structural.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[Renaming]\label{lem:rename}
  Let $\xi$ be a function on names with $\xi(\dead)=\dead$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\xi(\sigma_0)\lk{n}\xi(X)=\xi(\sigma_0\lk{n}X)$.
    \item $\runb{n}(\xi(\sigma),b)=\xi(\runb{n}(\sigma,b))$.
  \end{enumerate}
  The two sides are equal as traces.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \step{r1}{\assume{
      \begin{pfenum}
        \item $T$ is a trace, $\ell_1\neq\dead$, and $\xi'$ is $\xi$ with $\ell_2$ made $\ell_1$.
        \item No name $y\neq\ell_2$ with $\xi(y)=\ell_1$ is free in $T$.
        \item $k_1(\xi(w))=\xi(k_2(w))$ for each $w$.
      \end{pfenum}}
    \prove{$\killf{\ell_1}(\xi'(T))\bindt\lambda v.k_1(\closeloc{\ell_1}{v})=\xi(\killf{\ell_2}(T)\bindt\lambda v.k_2(\closeloc{\ell_2}{v}))$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \step{r11}{For each value $u$ of the spine of $T$, the kill of $\ell_1$ in $\xi'(u)$ is $\xi$ of the kill of $\ell_2$ in $u$.}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The 2 renamings agree on each name of $u$.
      They take $\ell_2$ to $\dead$.
      By assumption 2, $\xi$ takes no other name of $u$ to $\ell_1$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{r12}{For each leaf $v$ of $T$, $\closeloc{\ell_1}{\xi'(v)}=\xi(\closeloc{\ell_2}{v})$.}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By assumption 2, $\xi'$ takes only $\ell_2$ to $\ell_1$.
      So the close of $\ell_1$ after $\xi'$ is $\xi'$ after the close of $\ell_2$.
      After the close, $\ell_2$ is fresh, so $\xi'$ and $\xi$ give the same value.
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By \stepref{r11}, \stepref{r12} and assumption 3.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \step{r2}{\assume{The statements hold at the fuel $n$ for $\lnkv{n}$ and $\runb{n}$.}\prove{Statement 1 holds for $(x,\muval{v})\cons\sigma$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \step{r21}{\pflet{$\ell_1=\fresh(\xi(\sigma_0),\xi(v))$, $\ell_2=\fresh(\sigma_0,v)$, and $\xi'$ is $\xi$ with $\ell_2$ made $\ell_1$.}}
    \step{r22}{$\lnkv{n}(\xi(\sigma_0),\openloc{\ell_1}{\xi(v)})=\xi'(\lnkv{n}(\sigma_0,\openloc{\ell_2}{v}))$.}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ $\ell_2$ is fresh for $\sigma_0$ and for $v$.
      So $\xi'(\sigma_0)=\xi(\sigma_0)$ and $\xi'(\openloc{\ell_2}{v})=\openloc{\ell_1}{\xi(v)}$.
      Then use the assumption for $\xi'$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{r23}{No name $y\neq\ell_2$ with $\xi(y)=\ell_1$ is free in $\lnkv{n}(\sigma_0,\openloc{\ell_2}{v})$.}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ $\ell_1$ is fresh for $\xi(\sigma_0)$ and for $\xi(v)$.
      So such a $y$ is fresh for $\sigma_0$ and for $\openloc{\ell_2}{v}$, and $y\neq\dead$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh}, $y$ is fresh for the trace.
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By \stepref{r1}, \stepref{r22} and \stepref{r23}.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $n$, and then on $X$.
    The case of a recursive value is \stepref{r2}.
    In each other case, the renaming moves through $\bindt$.
    For statement 2, $\xi(\den{e}{n})=\den{e}{n}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh}, because $\xi(\dead)=\dead$.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}[Each fresh anchor]\label{cor:anchor}
  Let $\ell\neq\dead$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\ell$ is fresh for $\sigma_0$ and for $v$, then the equation of $\sigma_0\lk{n}(x,\muval{v})\cons\sigma$ holds with this $\ell$.
    \item If $\ell$ is fresh for $\sigma_0$, then $\sigma_0\splk{n}\killf{\ell}(t)=\killf{\ell}(\sigma_0\splk{n}t)$.
    \item The equation of $\den{\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}}{n}$ holds with $\ell$ in the place of the anchor $1$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{cor}
\begin{proof}
  \pf\ For statements 1 and 3, let $\xi'$ take the anchor of the equation to $\ell$ and keep each other name.
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:rename}, the trace with the anchor $\ell$ is the $\xi'$-renaming of the trace of the equation.
  Then use step $\langle1\rangle1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:rename} with the identity for $\xi$.
  For statement 2, let $\kappa$ take $\ell$ to $\dead$ and keep each other name.
  Then $\kappa(\sigma_0)=\sigma_0$.
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:rename}, linking into $\kappa(u)$ gives the $\kappa$-renaming of linking into $u$, for each guard $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[An anchor of the environment]\label{lem:subenv}
  Let $\ell\neq\dead$, and let $\muval{W}$ be a recursive value.
  If $\ell$ is fresh for $X$, then $\repl{(\sigma_0\lk{n}X)}{W}{\ell}\ext\repl{\sigma_0}{W}{\ell}\lk{n}X$.
  Also $\repl{\runb{n}(\sigma,b)}{W}{\ell}\ext\runb{n}(\repl{\sigma}{W}{\ell},b)$.
  Thus if $\runb{n}(\sigma,b)$ has the result $r$, then $\runb{n}(\repl{\sigma}{W}{\ell},b)$ has the result $\repl{r}{W}{\ell}$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \step{e1}{If $\sigma(x)=v$, then $\repl{\sigma}{W}{\ell}(x)=\repl{v}{W}{\ell}$.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $\sigma$.
    The replacement moves through unroll, because $\muval{W}$ is locally closed.
    The read of an entry $(x,\ell)$ is not defined, so this case does not occur.
  \end{proof}
  \step{e2}{The 2 inequalities hold.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $n$, and then on $X$.
    A read uses \stepref{e1}.
    When $\sigma(x)$ is not defined, the first trace is $\bot$, and each trace extends $\bot$.
    For a recursive value, open the 2 sides with one anchor that is fresh for $\sigma_0$, $\repl{\sigma_0}{W}{\ell}$, the value and $W$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}).
    The replacement moves through the kill and the close of that anchor.
    For a body, $\ell$ is fresh for $\den{e}{n}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:fresh}.
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By \stepref{e2} and Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[Unrolling]\label{lem:unroll}
  Let $\ell\neq\dead$ be fresh for $\Sigma_0$ and for $w$.
  Assume that $\lnkv{N}(\Sigma_0,\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has the result $w'$, and let $W=\closeloc{\ell}{w'}$.
  Then $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\unroll(\muval{w})$ has the result $\unroll(\muval{W})$, for each $m\ge 2N$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \step{u1}{$\unroll(\muval{w})=\repl{(\openloc{\ell}{w})}{w}{\ell}$ and $\unroll(\muval{W})=\repl{w'}{W}{\ell}$.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ $\ell$ is fresh for $w$, so to unroll is to open with $\ell$ and then to replace $\ell$.
    The value $w'$ is locally closed, so $\openloc{\ell}{W}=w'$.
  \end{proof}
  \step{u2}{For each $n$, $v$ and $r$: if $\lnkv{n}(\Sigma_0,v)$ has the result $r$, then $\lnkv{m}(\Sigma_0,\repl{v}{w}{\ell})$ has the result $\repl{r}{W}{\ell}$ for each $m\ge n+N$.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pfsketch\ By induction on $n$, and then on $v$.
    Each structural case uses the induction hypothesis on the parts.
    \step{u21}{\case{$v$ is the entry $(x,\ell)\cons\sigma$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The replaced entry is $(x,\muval{w})$.
      By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}, linking opens it with $\ell$.
      By the assumption and Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}, $\lnkv{m}(\Sigma_0,\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has the result $w'$.
      So the linked entry is $(x,\muval{W})$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{u22}{\case{$v$ is the entry $(x,\muval{u})\cons\sigma$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ Open with one anchor $\ell_2$ that is fresh for $\Sigma_0$, $u$, $w$ and $W$, and that is not $\ell$.
      The induction hypothesis on $n$ applies to $\openloc{\ell_2}{u}$.
      The replacement moves through the open and the close of $\ell_2$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{u23}{\case{$v$ is $\ReadE(S,x)$, and $S$ links to a module $\sigma$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the induction hypothesis, $\repl{S}{w}{\ell}$ links to $\repl{\sigma}{W}{\ell}$.
      By step $\langle1\rangle1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:subenv}, the read gives $\repl{\sigma(x)}{W}{\ell}$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{u24}{\case{$v$ is $\CallE(S,a)$, and $S$ links to a closure.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The call enters the body of the closure with an environment $\sigma$.
      By the induction hypothesis, the call after the replacement has the environment $\repl{\sigma}{W}{\ell}$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:subenv}, its result is the result of the first call with $\ell$ replaced.
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the cases, and by the induction hypothesis in each other case.
      In the case $\InitE$, $\ell$ is fresh for $\Sigma_0$.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By \stepref{u2} with $v=\openloc{\ell}{w}$, $n=N$ and $r=w'$, and by \stepref{u1}.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[Read]\label{lem:read}
  Assume that $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\sigma$ has the result $\sigma'$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\sigma(x)=v$ and $\Sigma_0\lk{m}v$ has the result $r$, then $\sigma'(x)=r$.
    \item If $\sigma'(x)=r$, then $\sigma(x)=v$ for some $v$, and from some fuel $\Sigma_0\lk{m'}v$ has the result $r$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
  \pf\ By induction on $\sigma$.
  The case of an entry $(x,\muval{w})$ needs an argument.
  Linking opens the entry with a fresh anchor $\ell$, and $\lnkv{m}(\Sigma_0,\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has a result $w'$.
  The linked entry is $(x,\muval{W})$ with $W=\closeloc{\ell}{w'}$.
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:unroll}, $\Sigma_0\lk{m'}\unroll(\muval{w})$ has the result $\unroll(\muval{W})$ for each $m'\ge 2m$.
  This is statement 2.
  For statement 1, the result $r$ at the fuel $m$ is the result at the fuel $2m$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{2 Linkings and 1 Linking}

Fix an environment $\Sigma_0$.
An environment $\sigma$ \emph{resolves to} $\sigma'$ when $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\sigma$ has the result $\sigma'$ at some fuel $m$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}, $\sigma$ resolves to at most one environment.
In a trace $\Sigma_0\lk{m}(\sigma\lk{n}X)$, the linking of $\sigma$ is the inner linking, and the linking of $\Sigma_0$ is the outer linking.

\begin{thm}[2 linkings and 1 linking]\label{thm:linklink}
  Assume that $\sigma$ resolves to $\sigma'$.
  Let $R_n(\sigma,X)$ be $\runb{n}(\sigma,b)$ for a body $X=b$, or $\lnkv{n}(\sigma,v)$ for a value $X=v$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] If $\Sigma_0\lk{m}R_n(\sigma,X)$ has the result $r$, then from some fuel $R_{m_2}(\sigma',X)$ has the result $r$.
          Its cost is at most $\cost(\Sigma_0\lk{m}R_n(\sigma,X))$.
    \item[(b)] If $R_{m'}(\sigma',X)$ has the result $r$, then from some pair of fuels $\Sigma_0\lk{m}R_n(\sigma,X)$ has the result $r$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
  \pfsketch\ Direction (a) is an induction on the fuel $n$ of the inner linking.
  Direction (b) is an induction on the fuel $m'$ of the 1 linking.
  Steps $\langle1\rangle1$ to $\langle1\rangle3$ prove (a), and step $\langle1\rangle4$ proves (b).
  \step{a1}{\assume{Direction (a) holds at the fuel $n$.}
    \prove{For each shadow, environment or value $X$: if $\Sigma_0\lk{m}(\sigma\lk{n}X)$ has the result $r$, then from some fuel $\sigma'\lk{m_2}X$ has the result $r$, at no more cost.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \pfsketch\ By induction on $X$.
    The outer linking of a trace $t\bindt k$ links into the spine of $t$, and then into $k(w)$ for the leaf $w$.
    \step{a11}{\case{$X=\InitE$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The inner trace is the leaf $\sigma$, so $r$ is the result of $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\sigma$.
      Thus $r=\sigma'$, and $\sigma'\lk{m_2}\InitE$ is the leaf $\sigma'$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{a12}{\case{$X=\ReadE(S,x)$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ Let $w$ be the leaf of the inner linking into $S$.
      If $w$ is a module $\sigma_1$, the inner trace ends with $\guardt{\sigma_1}{\sigma_1(x)}$.
      The outer linking resolves $\sigma_1$ to some $\sigma_1'$, and $\sigma_1(x)$ to $r$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:read}, $\sigma_1'(x)=r$.
      By the induction hypothesis, $\sigma'\lk{m_2}S$ has the result $\sigma_1'$.
      So $\sigma'\lk{m_2}\ReadE(S,x)$ ends with $\guardt{\sigma_1'}{r}$.
      If $w$ is a shadow, the outer linking makes the read.
      Then the induction hypothesis gives the same module on the 2 sides.
    \end{proof}
    \step{a13}{\case{$X=\CallE(S,v)$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ Let $f$ and $a$ be the leaves of the inner linkings into $S$ and $v$.
      The outer linking resolves them to some $f'$ and $a'$.
      By the induction hypothesis, $\sigma'\lk{m_2}S$ and $\sigma'\lk{m_2}v$ have the results $f'$ and $a'$.
      If $f$ is a shadow, the outer linking makes the call $\apf{m}(f',a')$, and more fuel keeps its result.
      If $f$ is a closure $\langle\lambda x.b,\sigma_1\rangle$, the inner linking entered $b$ with $(x,a)\cons\sigma_1$.
      This environment resolves to $(x,a')\cons\sigma_1'$, the environment of the call $\apf{m_2}(f',a')$.
      The assumption for the body $b$ gives the result $r$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{a14}{\case{$X=(x,\muval{w})\cons\sigma_1$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \step{a141}{\pflet{$\ell\neq\dead$ is fresh for $\Sigma_0$, $\sigma$, $\sigma'$ and $w$, and $T=\lnkv{n}(\sigma,\openloc{\ell}{w})$.}}
      \step{a142}{The outer linking into the spine of $\killf{\ell}(T)$ has a result $w_1$, the leaf of the spine of $T$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}, the inner linking opens with $\ell$, and the outer linking moves through $\killf{\ell}$.
      \end{proof}
      \step{a143}{$\Sigma_0\lk{m}T$ has a result $w_2$, and $r$ is $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{w_2}})\cons\sigma_2$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ The inner leaf is $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{w_1}})\cons\sigma_1''$.
        By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}, the outer linking opens it with $\ell$.
        The value $w_1$ is locally closed, so the opened value is $w_1$.
        So $\lnkv{m}(\Sigma_0,w_1)$ has a result $w_2$.
        By Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}, $\Sigma_0\lk{m}w_1$ has the same result.
      \end{proof}
      \qedstep
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By the assumption for the value $\openloc{\ell}{w}$ and \stepref{a143}, $\lnkv{m_2}(\sigma',\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has the result $w_2$ from some fuel.
        By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}, $\sigma'\lk{m_2}X$ opens with $\ell$.
        The induction hypothesis for $\sigma_1$ gives $\sigma_2$.
        The costs of the parts add on the 2 sides.
      \end{proof}
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the cases.
      Each other case uses the induction hypothesis on its parts.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \step{a2}{\assume{Direction (a) holds at the fuel $n$.}
    \prove{For each trace $t$: if $\Sigma_0\splk{m}(\sigma\splk{n}t)$ has the result $w$, then from some fuel $\sigma'\splk{m_2}t$ has the result $w$, at no more cost.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $t$.
    For a guard $\guardt{u}{t'}$, use \stepref{a1} for $u$.
    For a branch node, use \stepref{a1} for the scrutinee.
    If the inner linking gives a shadow, the outer linking selects the branch.
    If not, the inner linking selects the branch.
    The 1 linking selects the same branch in the 2 cases.
  \end{proof}
  \step{a3}{Direction (a) holds.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By induction on $n$.
    At the fuel $0$ the inner trace is $\bot$, and $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\bot$ is not finished.
    For a body at $n+1$, the inner trace is $\sigma\lk{n}b(n)$.
    Use \stepref{a2} for the spine of $b(n)$, and \stepref{a1} for its leaf.
    By Lemma~\ref{lem:fuel}, $b(m_2)$ extends $b(n)$ and gives the same finished trace.
    For a value at $n+1$, use \stepref{a1}.
  \end{proof}
  \step{b1}{Direction (b) holds.}
  \begin{proof}
    \pfsketch\ By induction on $m'$, with the same 3 steps as (a) in the inverse direction.
    The statement for a shadow, an environment or a value $X$ is:
    if $\sigma'\lk{m'}X$ has the result $r$, then from some pair of fuels $\Sigma_0\lk{m}(\sigma\lk{n}X)$ has the result $r$.
    \step{b11}{\case{$X=\ReadE(S,x)$, and $\sigma'\lk{m'}S$ has the result $\sigma_1'$ with $\sigma_1'(x)=r$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the induction hypothesis, from some pair of fuels the 2 linkings into $S$ have the result $\sigma_1'$.
      Let $w$ be the leaf of the inner linking.
      More fuel does not change $w$.
      If $w$ is a module $\sigma_1$, then $\sigma_1$ resolves to $\sigma_1'$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:read}, $\sigma_1(x)=v$ and $\Sigma_0\lk{m}v$ has the result $r$ from some fuel.
      So the outer linking into $\guardt{\sigma_1}{v}$ has the result $r$ from some fuel.
    \end{proof}
    \step{b12}{\case{$X=(x,\muval{w})\cons\sigma_1$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ Take $\ell$ as in step $\langle2\rangle4$ of (a).
      $\lnkv{m'}(\sigma',\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has a result $w_2$.
      By the induction hypothesis on $m'$, from some pair of fuels $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\lnkv{n}(\sigma,\openloc{\ell}{w})$ has the result $w_2$.
      Let $w_1$ be the leaf of its spine.
      The outer linking opens $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{w_1}})$ with $\ell$, and links into $w_1$ at the fuel $m-1$.
      So the statement holds from the pair of fuels with one more unit for $m$.
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the cases, and by the induction hypothesis in each other case.
      The case of a call that enters a body uses the induction hypothesis on $m'$.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ By \stepref{a3} and \stepref{b1}.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}[Calls]\label{cor:call}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] If $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\callt{n}(\sigma,b)$ has the result $r$, then $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\sigma$ has a result $\sigma'$.
          From some fuel, $\runb{m_2}(\sigma',b)$ has the result $r$ and a cost below $\cost(\Sigma_0\lk{m}\callt{n}(\sigma,b))$.
    \item[(b)] If $\sigma$ resolves to $\sigma'$ and $\runb{m'}(\sigma',b)$ has the result $r$, then from some pair of fuels $\Sigma_0\lk{m}\callt{n}(\sigma,b)$ has the result $r$.
  \end{enumerate}
  The same holds for $\apf{n}(f,a)$, with the results of $\Sigma_0\lk{m}f$ and $\Sigma_0\lk{m}a$.
\end{cor}
\begin{proof}
  \pf\ A call is the guard $\guardt{\sigma}{\runb{n}(\sigma,b)}$.
  The outer linking resolves $\sigma$, and then links into $\runb{n}(\sigma,b)$.
  Use Theorem~\ref{thm:linklink}.
  The guard gives the strict inequality of the costs.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Adequacy}

\begin{thm}[Adequacy]\label{thm:adequacy}
  Let $\rho$ be locally closed.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\evalf{N}(e,\rho)=u$, then $\runb{n}(\lift{\rho},\sembracket{e})$ has the result $\lift{u}$ for some $n$.
    \item If $\runb{n}(\lift{\rho},\sembracket{e})$ has the result $w$, then $\evalf{N}(e,\rho)=u$ for some $N$ and $u$, and $w=\lift{u}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
  \pfsketch\ In this proof $\Sigma_0$ is $\lift{\rho}$.
  A lifted value has no shadow.
  The trace $\runb{m+1}(\lift{\rho},\sembracket{e})$ is $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\den{e}{m}$.
  Statement 1 is an induction on $N$.
  Statement 2 is an induction on the cost of the finished trace.
  \step{w1}{\assume{$\evalf{N}(e,\rho)=u$, and statement 1 holds for each smaller fuel of the interpreter.}
    \prove{From some fuel, $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\den{e}{m}$ has the result $\lift{u}$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \step{w11}{\case{$e=x$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The trace is $\guardt{\lift{\rho}}{\lift{\rho}(x)}$.
      The lift moves through a read, so $\lift{\rho}(x)=\lift{(\rho(x))}$.
    \end{proof}
    \step{w12}{\case{$e=e_1\:e_2$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ The interpreter gives a closure $\langle\lambda x.e',\rho_1\rangle$ for $e_1$, a value $u_2$ for $e_2$, and $u$ for $e'$ in $(x,u_2)\cons\rho_1$.
      By the assumption, the traces of $e_1$ and $e_2$ have the lifts of these values as results.
      Let $f$ and $a$ be the leaves of their spines.
      By the assumption for $e'$, the call of the lifted closure at $\lift{u_2}$ has the result $\lift{u}$.
      By Corollary~\ref{cor:call}(b), $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\apf{m}(f,a)$ has the result $\lift{u}$ from some fuel.
    \end{proof}
    \step{w13}{\case{$e=\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \step{w131}{\pflet{$\ell=\fresh(\rho)$, $\evalf{N-1}(e_1,(x,\ell)\cons\rho)=u_1$, and $\evalf{N-1}(e_2,(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho)=\rho_2$.}}
      \step{w132}{The trace of the binding is the trace of Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}(3) with the anchor $\ell$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ $\ell\neq\dead$.
      \end{proof}
      \step{w133}{From some fuel, $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\callt{m}((x,\ell)\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e_1})$ has the result $\lift{u_1}$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ The environment $(x,\ell)\cons[\InitE]$ resolves to $\lift{((x,\ell)\cons\rho)}$.
        Use the assumption for $e_1$ and Corollary~\ref{cor:call}(b).
      \end{proof}
      \step{w134}{\pflet{$v_1$ is the leaf of the spine of the trace of \stepref{w133}. Then $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}v_1$ has the result $\lift{u_1}$.}}
      \step{w135}{The environment $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{v_1}})\cons[\InitE]$ resolves to $\lift{((x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho)}$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ $\ell$ is fresh for $\lift{\rho}$.
        By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}, linking opens the entry with $\ell$, which gives $v_1$.
        Use \stepref{w134}.
        The lift moves through the close.
      \end{proof}
      \step{w136}{From some fuel, $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\callt{m}((x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{v_1}})\cons[\InitE],\sembracket{e_2})$ has the result $\lift{\rho_2}$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By \stepref{w135}, the assumption for $e_2$ and Corollary~\ref{cor:call}(b).
      \end{proof}
      \qedstep
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}(2), the spine of the killed call of $e_1$ links to the leaf $v_1$.
        By \stepref{w136}, the leaf $m$ of the call of $e_2$ resolves to $\lift{\rho_2}$.
        The last leaf is $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{v_1}})\cons\modf(m)$.
        By \stepref{w135} it links to $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{\lift{u_1}}})\cons\lift{\rho_2}$, the lift of the value of the interpreter.
      \end{proof}
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the cases.
      A link term and a match enter a body as in \stepref{w12}.
      A function, the empty module and a constructor have no call.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \step{w2}{\assume{$\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\den{e}{m}$ has the result $w$ and the cost $c$, and statement 2 holds for each trace of a smaller cost.}
    \prove{$\evalf{N}(e,\rho)=u$ for some $N$ and $u$, and $w=\lift{u}$.}}
  \begin{proof}
    \pfsketch\ By induction on $e$, for each $w$ and each cost of at most $c$.
    A part of $e$ that runs in the same environment has a trace of at most the same cost.
    \step{w21}{\assume{$\lift{\rho}\lk{m}\callt{m}(\sigma,\sembracket{e'})$ has the result $w'$ at a cost of at most $c$, and $\sigma$ resolves to $\lift{\rho'}$.}
      \prove{$\evalf{N}(e',\rho')=u'$ for some $N$ and $u'$, and $w'=\lift{u'}$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By Corollary~\ref{cor:call}(a), $\runb{m_2}(\lift{\rho'},\sembracket{e'})$ has the result $w'$ at a cost below $c$.
      Use the assumption.
    \end{proof}
    \step{w22}{\case{$e=e_1\:e_2$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ Let $f$ and $a$ be the leaves of the spines of $e_1$ and $e_2$.
      By Corollary~\ref{cor:call}(a), linking resolves them to some $f'$ and $a'$, and $\apf{m_2}(f',a')$ has the result $w$ at no more cost.
      By the induction hypothesis on $e$, $f'$ and $a'$ are the lifts of values of the interpreter.
      So $f'$ is a lifted closure, and $\apf{m_2}(f',a')$ is a call behind a guard.
      Its body has a smaller cost, and the assumption applies.
    \end{proof}
    \step{w23}{\case{$e=\synbind{x}{e_1}{e_2}$.}}
    \begin{proof}
      \step{w231}{\pflet{$\ell=\fresh(\rho)$, and the trace of the binding is the trace with the anchor $\ell$.}}
      \step{w232}{The spine of the call of $e_1$ has a leaf $v_1$, and $\lift{\rho}\lk{m}v_1$ has a result $v_1'$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By Corollary~\ref{cor:anchor}(2), the outer linking moves through $\killf{\ell}$.
        The call of $e_2$ starts with a guard on $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{v_1}})\cons[\InitE]$.
        Linking opens this entry with $\ell$, and resolves $v_1$.
      \end{proof}
      \step{w233}{$\evalf{N_1}(e_1,(x,\ell)\cons\rho)=u_1$ for some $N_1$ and $u_1$, and $v_1'=\lift{u_1}$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By \stepref{w232}, the linked call of $e_1$ is finished with the result $v_1'$.
        Its cost is below the cost of the whole trace, because the guard of the call of $e_2$ follows it.
        Use \stepref{w21}.
      \end{proof}
      \step{w234}{$\evalf{N_2}(e_2,(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho)=\rho_2$ for some $N_2$ and $\rho_2$.}
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By \stepref{w233}, the environment of the call of $e_2$ resolves to the lift of $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho$.
        Use \stepref{w21}.
        The result is a module, because the last leaf takes a module.
      \end{proof}
      \qedstep
      \begin{proof}
        \pf\ By \stepref{w233} and \stepref{w234}, the interpreter gives $(x,\muval{\closeloc{\ell}{u_1}})\cons\rho_2$.
        The last leaf links to its lift, as in the forward direction.
      \end{proof}
    \end{proof}
    \qedstep
    \begin{proof}
      \pf\ By the cases.
      A link term and a match use \stepref{w21}.
      A variable reads a lifted value.
    \end{proof}
  \end{proof}
  \qedstep
  \begin{proof}
    \pf\ Statement 1 is \stepref{w1} by induction on $N$.
    Statement 2 is \stepref{w2} by induction on the cost.
  \end{proof}
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}[Termination]\label{cor:termination}
  A closed term $e$ has a value in the interpreter exactly when its denotation has a result in the empty environment.
  That is, $\evalf{N}(e,\bullet)$ is defined for some $N$ exactly when $\runb{n}(\bullet,\sembracket{e})$ is finished for some $n$.
\end{cor}
\begin{proof}
  \pf\ By Theorem~\ref{thm:adequacy}.
  The empty environment is locally closed.
\end{proof}
